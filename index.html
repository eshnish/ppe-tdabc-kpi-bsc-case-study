Profit Per Employee, TDABC, KPI Design & Balanced Scorecard

A cost-allocation and performance-management framework built to turn department-level financial data into board-ready, decision-grade insight — designed and implemented as part of an active Business Analyst / Business Analytics role.

Muhamad Nishad · Business Analyst — Business Growth & Financial Strategy · eshnish90@gmail.com · LinkedIn
Note: this page documents a methodology and implementation approach built on the job. Company name, exact figures, and department identifiers are generalized/illustrative to respect employer confidentiality — the framework, formulas, and design logic are accurate representations of the work.

Executive Summary

Most departmental profitability reporting relies on flat, averaged cost allocation — every employee costs "the same," regardless of what they actually do. That approach hides where profit is really being made or lost. This project replaces flat allocation with a Time-Driven Activity-Based Costing (TDABC) model feeding a Profit Per Employee (PPE) framework, structures the resulting metrics through a disciplined KPI design methodology, and ties all of it to strategy through a Balanced Scorecard — so every number on a dashboard can be traced back to a decision it's meant to support.

The result is a single, connected pipeline: activity data → true cost-to-profit figures → validated KPIs → strategic scorecard → board-ready dashboard — rather than four disconnected spreadsheets built separately each reporting cycle.

Business Context

Built against an active Business Analyst charter covering three specific mandates:

  • PPE ownership — deriving granular, resource-level profitability across every business unit.
  • KPI Master Sheet development — designing and maintaining the organization's core performance metrics.
  • Financial analysis & forecasting — rolling forecasts and department-level cost/variance tracking.

The gap this project addresses: these three mandates existed as separate deliverables. This implementation connects them into one traceable system.

Data & Framework Architecture

ERP / Time & Cost Data→ TDABC Cost Allocation→ PPE Model→ KPI Design & Validation→ Balanced Scorecard→ Power BI Dashboard

Each stage is a deliberate handoff, not a rebuild: TDABC produces the defensible cost figures that PPE needs; PPE produces the raw metrics that get filtered through KPI design discipline; validated KPIs are what actually populate the Balanced Scorecard's four perspectives; the scorecard is what the dashboard visualizes. Nothing enters the board dashboard that hasn't passed through this chain.

PPE — Profit Per Employee Framework

PPE reframes department profitability from a top-line, per-department number into a per-resource figure — because two departments with identical revenue can have very different profit efficiency depending on staffing intensity and activity cost.

Before: Department profit ÷ department headcount (flat average).
After: Department profit ÷ resource-level activity cost derived from TDABC — see below.

This is the layer that makes the metric defensible in front of a CFO: it answers "why" a department's PPE moved, not just "that" it moved.

TDABC — Time-Driven Activity-Based Costing Methodology + Worked Example

TDABC replaces flat manpower cost allocation with a capacity cost rate derived from actual resource supply and practical capacity — the same approach Kaplan & Anderson designed to make ABC costing usable at scale without exploding the model's complexity.

Core formula

Capacity Cost Rate = Cost of Resources Supplied ÷ Practical Capacity of Resources Supplied

Illustrative worked example

InputIllustrative value
Monthly department cost (salaries, overhead, tools)SAR 180,000
Headcount6 employees
Practical capacity per employee (accounting for non-productive time)120 hours/month (of 160 available)
Total practical capacity720 hours/month
Capacity Cost RateSAR 250/hour (180,000 ÷ 720)

Once the capacity cost rate is established, a time equation assigns cost to each activity based on how long it actually takes — so a department handling high-complexity, high-touch work is costed accurately against one that handles high-volume, low-touch work, instead of both being averaged the same.

Activity Cost = Capacity Cost Rate × Time Required for Activity

This activity cost is what feeds the PPE model above — replacing the flat average with a figure that reflects what the department actually does.

KPI Design — KRI / PI / KPI Discipline

Not every metric on a dashboard deserves to be called a KPI. This project applies Parmenter's tiering discipline to separate lagging results indicators from true performance drivers, so the KPI Master Sheet stops accumulating vanity metrics.

TierDefinitionExample (illustrative)
KRI — Key Result IndicatorTells you how you did on a critical success factor — a summary, not actionable day-to-dayQuarterly departmental profit
PI — Performance IndicatorTells you what to do — actionable, but not critical enough to report to the board dailyWeekly billable-hours utilization
KPI — Key Performance IndicatorTells you what to do to increase performance dramatically — the vital few, monitored continuouslyDaily capacity cost rate variance vs. budget

Validation layer: before a metric is promoted to KPI status, it's checked against outcomes it claims to predict — a metric only earns "leading indicator" status if it demonstrably correlates with the result it's meant to forecast, not by assumption.

Balanced Scorecard — Four-Perspective Map

The Balanced Scorecard is what stops the KPI Master Sheet from becoming a list of disconnected operational metrics — every KPI has to earn its place by tying back to one of four strategic perspectives.

Financial

PPE, capacity cost rate variance, rolling forecast accuracy, cost-to-profit ratio by department.

Customer

Opportunity-to-sales conversion, service-level adherence, client retention by segment.

Internal Process

TDABC time-equation accuracy, activity cycle time, process-exception rate, department cash-burn trend.

Learning & Growth

Cross-department dashboard adoption, forecasting-model iteration rate, data-quality score of source systems.

Strategy map logic

Learning & Growth→ Internal Process→ Customer→ Financial

Read left to right: improvements in data quality and dashboard adoption (Learning & Growth) drive process accuracy and cycle-time improvements (Internal Process), which improve conversion and retention (Customer), which ultimately shows up in PPE and cost-to-profit figures (Financial) — the causal chain a strategy map is meant to make explicit.

Tech Stack

Power BI PostgreSQL / SQL Power Query / M Python (pandas) ERP integration DAX time-intelligence Row-Level Security (RLS) Star Schema Modeling

SQL pulls and shapes department cost/time data; Power Query handles the TDABC cost-rate refresh; the underlying dataset is modeled as a star schema (fact table for activity cost/time, dimension tables for department, cost center, and date) so DAX time-intelligence measures can drive the rolling-forecast and variance views without performance issues. Row-Level Security (RLS) roles enforce the department-centric access boundary at the model level — the same safeguard referenced under Governance below — rather than relying on separate reports per department. Python (pandas) is used for the heavier cost-driver and KPI-validation analysis that doesn't belong inside the report layer.

Certification alignment: the data-prep, star-schema modeling, DAX, and RLS work in this project maps directly onto the four domains of the Microsoft Certified: Power BI Data Analyst Associate (PL-300) exam — this case study is the applied, on-the-job proof behind that credential, not just exam theory.

Governance & Documentation

Every metric on the scorecard traces back through a documented chain: source system → TDABC calculation → KPI tier classification → BSC perspective → dashboard visual. This traceability handbook is what makes the framework audit-ready rather than dependent on one person's institutional memory — a deliberate design choice, not an afterthought added for compliance.
